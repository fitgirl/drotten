# My favourite ship

**M/S Drotten is the best ship** because somebody put **50,400 kW of machinery** inside a 196-metre passenger ferry and I apparently need an entire website to explain how much I love that. She does 28.5 knots. That’s about 52.8 km/h. Look at her **SIZE** and then think about that speed again. [Actual ship numbers](https://www.destinationgotland.se/en/all-about-the-trip/our-ferries/).

This is my Drotten site. The specification list is compulsory. scroll slowly.

## The numbers

Those figures come from [Gotlandsbolaget’s fleet details](https://gotlandsbolaget.se/om-oss/vara-fartyg/). I keep the decimals because 25.67 is a perfectly good number and rounding it feels unnecessary.

- **Built:** 2003. Guangzhou Shipyard International, China
- **Ship type:** Ro-ro passenger ferry. Passengers upstairs, vehicles rolling on and off the vehicle decks.
- **Length overall:** 196 metres
- **Between perpendiculars:** 176 metres. Different measurement, yes I want both.
- **Beam:** 25.67 metres
- **Draught:** 6.40 metres
- **Service speed:** 28.5 knots. Approximately 52.8 km/h
- **Main machinery output:** 50,400 kW. 50.4 MEGAWATTS.
- **Auxiliary machinery:** Another 4,560 kW
- **Passenger capacity:** 1,500 people
- **Vehicle capacity:** 500 cars. Or 1,800 lane metres of cargo space
- **Gross tonnage:** 29,746 GT. This measures internal volume; it isn’t the ship’s weight.
- **Deadweight:** 5,174 tonnes
- **Ice class:** 1A
- **IMO number:** 9223796
- **Call sign:** SGPI

## Four. Wärtsilä. Engines.

- **Engine type:** Wärtsilä 46-series diesels. Wärtsilä supplied eight main engines across the two sister ships.
- **Drotten’s exact engine listing:** **four Wärtsilä 12V46C engines.** Four. I could have put this beside the power figure but then you might have scrolled past it.

**50,400 kW. 50.4 MEGAWATTS.**

## The Gotland name thing

Drotten was **M/S Gotland from 2003 to 2020**, so finding an old photo with GOTLAND on her is like finding another bit of her photo album. I will open one picture to check the name and suddenly there are fourteen tabs and it’s late. Same ship, earlier name, years of crossings already behind her.

This is why I care about **IMO 9223796** so much: names move around, and I want to know which actual hull I’m looking at. The 2003 Gotland became Drotten in 2020. I keep both names in my searches because otherwise you miss **SEVENTEEN YEARS** of her history.

[Drotten’s name history and old photographs](https://www.ferry-site.dk/ferry.php?id=9223796).
