# About Drotten

Drotten is my favourite ferry. I like the shape of the bridge and the long windows along the side, but mostly I am interested in the machinery. Four Wärtsilä engines and 50,400 kW in total. Quite a lot for a boat which many people only think about when they need to go to Gotland.

I made this page to collect what I find about her. Most information is from Swedish pages so I have written it in English here. Some ship terms are easier in Swedish for me. If I write *bredd* it means beam, and *djupgående* means draught.

## Ship facts

These are the figures in [Gotlandsbolaget’s fleet list](https://gotlandsbolaget.se/om-oss/vara-fartyg/). I use their measurements here.

- **Built:** 2003
- **Shipyard:** Guangzhou Shipyard International, China
- **Type:** ro-ro passenger ferry
- **Length overall:** 196 m
- **Length between perpendiculars:** 176 m
- **Beam:** 25.67 m
- **Draught:** 6.40 m
- **Gross tonnage:** 29,746 GT
- **Net tonnage:** 9,505 NT
- **Deadweight:** 5,174 tonnes
- **Speed listed:** 28.5 knots
- **Main machinery:** 50,400 kW
- **Auxiliary machinery:** 4,560 kW
- **Passengers:** 1,500
- **Cabins:** 115
- **Vehicle capacity:** 500 cars or 1,800 lane metres
- **Classification:** DNV
- **Ice class:** 1A
- **Call sign:** SGPI
- **IMO number:** 9223796
- **Exhaust treatment:** Siemens SINOx SCR catalytic converters
- **NOx figure in the owner’s list:** maximum 2 g/kWh

Destination Gotland lists her as **SF1500**. Her Swedish routes connect Visby with Nynäshamn and Oskarshamn. The newer Gotland and Visby are listed as SF1650, so make sure you open the SF1500 information when looking for Drotten. [Operator’s ship list](https://www.destinationgotland.se/en/all-about-the-trip/our-ferries/).

Some other numbers from [The Ferry Site](https://www.ferry-site.dk/ferry.php?id=9223796):

- **Yard number:** 30005
- **Flag:** Sweden
- **Port of registry:** Visby
- **Engine model:** Wärtsilä 12V46C
- **Number of main engines:** four
- **Sister ship:** Visborg, the ship originally called Visby from 2003

One thing about the measurements. The Ferry Site gives 195.8 m length and 25.0 m breadth, while Gotlandsbolaget gives 196 m and 25.67 m. It also has a different draught and lane-metre figure. I have not found an explanation for all of these differences, so I keep the owner’s figures together instead of choosing numbers from both lists.

## Engines

The four main engines are **Wärtsilä 12V46C diesels**. I really like this part of the ship. You see the lounges and restaurant when travelling but below there is machinery producing 50.4 MW.

Wärtsilä’s own delivery statement says they supplied **eight Wärtsilä 46 main engines and six Wärtsilä 20 auxiliary engines for the two ships together**. They also supplied the propellers for one of the ships. The statement does not say which one, so I cannot use it to say that Drotten definitely has their propellers. [Wärtsilä’s delivery statement](https://www.wartsila.com/media/news/29-08-2003-statement-by-wartsila-on-the-gotland-bribery-suspect).

A few calculations from the published figures:

- 50,400 kW divided by four is **12,600 kW per main engine**, if the total is shared equally.
- 50,400 kW is **50.4 MW**.
- 28.5 knots is **52.8 km/h**, rounded to one decimal.
- The four main engines together have about **11 times** the listed auxiliary power.

These are calculations, not extra measurements from a sea trial. Also 28.5 knots is the listed speed. It does not mean she goes that fast on every crossing.

## Who designed her

**KNUD E. HANSEN** worked on the design of the 2003 Visby and Gotland. Their project page says both ships were delivered in 2003 and built at Guangzhou Shipyard International.

Their work included the hull structure, accommodation, machinery, electrical design and piping. They also worked on model tests, noise and vibration analysis, trials and supervision at the yard. I find the noise part interesting because all this machinery still has to work under rooms where people want to sit and sleep.

[The designer’s project page](https://www.knudehansen.com/reference/visby/) is called “Visby”, but it also identifies Gotland as the sister ship. This is the old Gotland which became Drotten.

## Inside the ship

I found the [official SF1500 seating plan](https://www.destinationgotland.se/globalassets/dokument/oversiktskartor/sf1500-seatingplan.pdf). It is much more useful than guessing from photos.

- **Deck 7** is marked as the lounge deck, *salongdäck*.
- **Deck 8** is marked as the day-cabin deck, *kupédäck*.
- The plan lists a front lounge and an aft lounge.
- There is a separate pet lounge, *djursalong*.
- It also lists the information desk, bistro, restaurant and shop.
- There is a playroom and a children’s cinema.
- Day cabins, a bar and a sun deck are shown in the plan.
- The staircases are labelled **A, B and C**.

The Swedish word *kupé* is translated as “day cabin” on this plan. I mention this because the wording can be confusing if you are comparing different ferry websites.

## When she was called Gotland

She was called **M/S Gotland from 2003 until 2020**. After that she became Drotten. So if you find a picture from 2010 with GOTLAND written on this ship, that is correct.

There have been several ships with the name Gotland. I check the build year and IMO number before saving a fact. This one is **2003, IMO 9223796**. The name can change but that number still identifies the same ship.

Her sister was originally the 2003 Visby and is now listed as Visborg. The newer ships also use the names Gotland and Visby. Honestly the names are more difficult to keep track of than the engine numbers.

[The Ferry Site’s name history and photos](https://www.ferry-site.dk/ferry.php?id=9223796).

## Places she has sailed

Drotten has done more than the Gotland crossings.

- **2003:** built as Gotland and joined the Gotland service. [Gotlandsbolaget](https://gotlandsbolaget.se/fartyg/m-s-drotten/).
- **2020:** changed name from Gotland to Drotten. [Name history](https://www.ferry-site.dk/ferry.php?id=9223796).
- **27 January 2021:** the Port of Dunkerque announced Drotten for the **Dunkerque–Rosslare** service operated by DFDS. That is France to Ireland. The port also described her as Visby’s sister ship. [Port announcement](https://www.dunkerque-port.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/403_Irlande_Drotten_EN.pdf).
- **30 August 2021:** Hansa Destinations started its Nynäshamn–Rostock service. Drotten was used on this operation, which also included Visby calls. [Gotlandsbolaget’s announcement](https://corporate.gotlandsbolaget.se/sv/hansa-destinations-trafikerar-inte-nynashamn-rostock-under-2023/).
- **15 December 2022:** the company announced that Hansa Destinations would not operate the route in 2023. They gave high fuel prices and difficulty increasing freight volumes as the reasons. This was a decision about the route, not Drotten being scrapped. [Same announcement](https://corporate.gotlandsbolaget.se/sv/hansa-destinations-trafikerar-inte-nynashamn-rostock-under-2023/).
- **August 2023:** Gotlandsbolaget reported technical problems which took Drotten out of Gotland service for repairs at the end of week 32. The report does not explain the exact fault, so I have not written a guess here. [Company report](https://corporate.gotlandsbolaget.se/sv/delarsrapport-januari-juni-2023/).

I think the France–Ireland period is especially interesting. When I think of this ship I think of Visby, so it feels strange seeing the same ferry working so far from Gotland.
